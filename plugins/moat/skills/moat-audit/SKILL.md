---
name: moat-audit
description: Audit a finished result, report, log entry or paper draft before it is believed — hunting bare numbers, missing thresholds, unread arms in the headline, pooled populations and relaxed bars. Use on a draft write-up, a results table, or a claim someone is about to act on.
---

# moat-audit

Read the target and report what cannot be trusted as written. This is a reading
pass, not a re-run: everything below is answerable from the text and its
registration.

Walk these in order and quote the offending line for each hit:

1. **Bare numbers.** Every figure must carry its quantity, its configuration and
   its bar. Flag any number whose *unit* is ambiguous between shares of
   different populations, and any figure that appears in two places with two
   definitions.
2. **Verdict words without thresholds.** "significant", "localised",
   "saturated", "large", "holds", "passes" — each must name the number it was
   judged against. A judgement word travels with its bar or not at all.
3. **Unread arms in the headline.** Any arm that failed an instrument check or a
   negative control must not appear in the summary, the abstract or the lead
   table without the words that say it failed. Check whether the best-looking
   number in the write-up belongs to an arm the registration excludes.
4. **Order of presentation.** Checks and controls must print before the primary
   value. If the reader meets the result first, the controls are decoration.
5. **Pooling.** Any mean across populations that are different tasks; any
   average that includes near-empty units; any micro-average that does not say
   what it pools over; any pair of numbers averaged because their means agreed.
6. **Bars that moved.** Compare every criterion in the write-up against the
   registration, by text. A bar that changed after scores existed invalidates
   the criterion, and the honest form is to report against both.
7. **Missing trivial arms.** Do-nothing, do-everything, existing-tool-alone, and
   a no-training table over one cheap descriptor. If any is absent, the result
   has no floor. If do-everything's score is an identity, that must be stated.
8. **Unanchored instruments.** Find the cell whose value came from outside the
   pipeline. If every check compares two parts of the same pipeline, the whole
   report is one self-consistent system and its orientation is unestablished.
9. **Stale open questions.** A sentence that asks for a check the document
   already answers elsewhere. These accumulate silently and invite re-runs that
   are already done.
10. **Retractions that did not propagate.** Any withdrawn number still quoted
    anywhere, and any store that was not corrected in the same sitting.

Output: the findings, most load-bearing first, each with the line and the
smallest repair. Then say plainly which claims in the document survive the pass
and which do not — including "all of them" when that is the answer.
